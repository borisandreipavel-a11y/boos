# Pictures: a picture file turned into dots

Code: the folder `pictures/`, a crate of this project's own: `src/lib.rs` (what it offers, its limits, its reasons), `src/png.rs` (PNG pictures), `src/unpack.rs` (unpacking: zlib and DEFLATE), `src/jpeg.rs` (JPEG photos), `src/entropy.rs` (a JPEG's packed data), `src/transform.rs` (a JPEG's transforms and colours), `src/shrink.rs` (shrinking to fit a room), `src/check.rs` (the two check values). Its tests: `src/tests.rs`, `src/jpeg_tests.rs`, `tests/memory.rs` and `test-data/`, run by `test-tools/test_pictures.py` (see "How it is tested" below).

It reads a PNG picture or a JPEG photo from the bytes of its file and gives back its dots, shrunk to fit a room of a given size, ready to paint. The kernel uses it to show kept PNG pictures and JPEG photos in the frame beside the panel of apps (see "In the kernel" below, and "Pictures" in [screen.md](screen.md)).

## The words

- **Dots** are what the screen shows (pixels). A picture here is a grid of dots, four bytes each: red, green, blue, and **alpha**, how much the dot covers what lies behind it, from 0 (not at all) to 255 (wholly).
- **Packed** bytes are bytes made smaller with DEFLATE; **unpacking** turns them back into the bytes they were. A PNG's dots are packed so, and so are most streams of a PDF and the parts of Word and Excel files.
- A **sample** is one number of a dot as the file keeps it: a grey, a red, an alpha, or the number of a palette's colour.
- A **room** is the size, in dots, that a picture must fit into: for the kernel, the frame beside the panel of apps.
- Of a JPEG photo: a **component** is one of its planes of samples (grey; or Y, Cb and Cr, brightness and two colour differences); a **block** is 8 by 8 samples of a component, sent as the 64 **coefficients** of its cosine transform; a **unit** (T.81's MCU) is the blocks of each component that cover the same part of the photo; a **scan** is one pass over the photo's data, of some components and some coefficients.

## Why a crate of its own

- **To run fast.** The kernel's own code is built the plain way, and the outside crates and `password-key/` are built to run fast (`opt-level = 2` in the kernel's `Cargo.toml`); built plain they were 2 to 25 times slower. Reading a picture is heavy work of the same kind, so it lives in a crate of its own, as `password-key/` does. The checks for a number too large for its type stay on.
- **It uses no other crate**, has no build script and is not a macro crate. It needs `core` and `alloc` only (`#![no_std]`), and whole numbers only: no arithmetic with a point, which this target does in software, and nothing that could use the processor's SSE registers, which the kernel refuses.
- **Nothing in it may stop the kernel.** Every byte of a picture comes from outside, and may be damaged or made to do harm. So:
  - it has no unsafe code (`#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`);
  - clippy turns into an error any indexing that could fail, `unwrap`, `expect`, `panic!` and the like (the list is at the top of `src/lib.rs`); reads go through `get`, which can only come back empty;
  - every size is checked before memory is set aside for it (see "Memory" below);
  - nothing large is put on the stack, and no function calls itself, since the kernel's main loop runs on the firmware's stack of 128 KB, where nothing notices an overflow (see [memory.md](memory.md)). The largest lists on the stack are 318 bytes of code lengths and 288 of the fixed code's for a PNG, and for a JPEG its tables, under 3 KB (their quick tables are on the heap), and a block's 512 bytes of sums.
  A picture it cannot read is turned away with the reason in plain words (`Refusal`). A mistake in its code that the checks did not stop would still end in a stop of the kernel, the red screen, through Rust's own checks, never in a write outside its memory: that is what the tests that feed it damaged files are for.

## What it offers

- **`png_head(file)`** reads the signature and the head only, and gives the picture's width and height in dots, its depth and its kind of colour, and whether it is interlaced: for saying how large a picture is without reading it all.
- **`png_picture(file, room_width, room_height, &limits)`** reads the whole picture and gives a `Picture`: its width and height as shown, and its dots, row after row from the top, four bytes each. The colours are not mixed with any background, so that the same dots can be painted over another look's colour without the file being read again. Or it gives a `Refusal`.
- **`jpeg_head(file)`** reads the segments up to the frame, and gives the photo's width and height as stored, how many components it has (1 or 3), whether it is progressive, and the turn its Exif note gives (`turn`, 1 when none); `seen()` gives its size as it is seen, the sides swapped for a quarter turn.
- **`jpeg_picture(file, room_width, room_height, &jpeg_limits)`** reads a whole JPEG photo, as `png_picture` a PNG, and turns it as its Exif note says (see "The turn" below).
- **`fitted_size(width, height, room_width, room_height)`**: the size a picture is shown at (see "Shrinking" below).
- **`Limits`**, given by the caller, who alone knows how much memory there is: `dots_most`, the most dots a picture may have; `side_most`, the most on either side; `interlaced_dots_most`, the most an interlaced picture may have, since such a picture is put together whole before it is shrunk.
- **`JpegLimits`**, the same for a JPEG photo: `dots_most` and `side_most`, as for a PNG (every block of a photo is read even when it is shown small, so `dots_most` also bounds the time it takes); and `held_most`, the most bytes a photo that comes in several scans may keep for its blocks (see "JPEG" below).
- **`Refusal`** and **`because()`**, the reason in words to stand after "because": `it does not begin as a PNG picture does`; `it does not begin as a JPEG photo does`; `it is a kind of JPEG I do not read: it is lossless`; `the file ends before the picture does`; `it is damaged: ...`; `it holds a part I cannot read, "ABCD"`; `it is 30000 by 30000 dots, more than I show`; `there is no room to show it`.

## PNG

What it follows: RFC 2083 (PNG 1.0), the text the W3C's later editions grew from. The third edition (a W3C Recommendation of 24 June 2025) still has only the four parts below that a reader must know; the rest, its animation among them, may be passed over, and then "a non-animated PNG decoder will ignore the ancillary APNG-specific chunks and display the static image".

- **The file** begins with the bytes 137 80 78 71 13 10 26 10, and is then a row of parts ("chunks"): a length of at most 2^31 - 1, a name of four letters, the data, and a CRC-32 of the name and the data. A part whose name begins with a small letter may be passed over; every other part a reader must know: IHDR (the head), PLTE (a palette), IDAT (the packed dots) and IEND (the end). Anything after the end part is not read.
- **What is checked in every part:** that it is whole, that its check value fits, and that its name is four letters. A part whose name begins with a capital and that is none of the four is turned down by its name.
- **The head** comes first, 13 bytes: width and height, each from 1 to 2^31 - 1; the depth and the kind of colour, in the pairs RFC 2083 allows (grey: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 bits; red-green-blue: 8, 16; a palette's colours: 1, 2, 4, 8; grey with alpha: 8, 16; red-green-blue with alpha: 8, 16); packing 0 (deflate); filtering 0; interlacing 0 (none) or 1 (Adam7).
- **The order:** one head, first; at most one palette, before the dots and before the transparency part, only where a palette may be (never with grey), and needed with a palette's colours, with at most as many colours as the depth can name; the transparency part (tRNS) before the dots, after the palette, at most once, two bytes for grey, six for red-green-blue, no more than the palette's colours for a palette, and not where every dot has its own alpha; the IDAT parts in one run, their boundaries anywhere and some of them empty if so made; the end part last, empty.
- **The packed dots** are the IDAT parts' data one after another, unpacked as one zlib stream (see "Unpacking" below). Their bytes are rows: for each row a filter byte and the row's bytes, a row of dots of fewer than eight bits packed several to a byte, the leftmost in the highest bits, each row beginning on a byte. Exactly as many bytes must come as the head's size says; more or fewer is damage.
- **Filters** (RFC 2083, 6): each byte was stored as its difference from a guess made from the byte a whole dot to its left (at least one byte), the byte above it, and the one above that left one, all counting as 0 left of the row and above the first row of each pass: none, the left byte, the byte above, their average rounded down, or Paeth's guess, whose ties go to the left byte, then the one above. A filter byte above 4 is damage.
- **Adam7** sends an interlaced picture in seven passes, each laid out as a small picture of its own, with its own rows and filters; a pass a small picture has no dots of is left out with no bytes at all. Where each pass begins and steps, across and down: (0, 0, 8, 8), (4, 0, 8, 8), (0, 4, 4, 8), (2, 0, 4, 4), (0, 2, 2, 4), (1, 0, 2, 2), (0, 1, 1, 2), read from RFC 2083's pattern (2.6).
- **Four bytes a dot:** samples of 1, 2 or 4 bits are stretched to the full range (times 255 and divided by the largest sample); samples of 16 bits keep their high byte, as RFC 2083 allows (10.4); grey becomes equal red, green and blue; a palette's colour is looked up, and a dot that names a colour the palette does not have is damage; its alpha is the transparency part's for that colour, or 255. For grey and red-green-blue, a dot whose samples are those of the transparency part is see-through (alpha 0); the match is made on the samples as they are, before they are cut to 8 bits.
- **Not applied:** gamma (gAMA), the colour of the screen it was made on (cHRM, iCCP, sRGB, cICP) and the other parts that may be passed over. The colours are shown as they are written.

## Unpacking

What it follows: RFC 1950 (zlib) and RFC 1951 (DEFLATE). Bytes come from a `Source` one at a time and go to a `Sink` a piece at a time, so the whole of neither is ever held.

- **The zlib head** must check (the two bytes, read as one number, divide by 31), name deflate (8), a window of at most 32 KB, and no preset dictionary, which a PNG never has. At the end, the Adler-32 of everything unpacked must fit. Bytes after the stream are not read.
- **Blocks** (RFC 1951, 3.2.3): stored (its bytes as they are, after a length and that length with every bit turned over, which must agree), packed with the fixed codes, or packed with codes the block gives itself. The fourth kind does not exist and is damage.
- **Codes** are built from lengths alone (3.2.2): the codes of each length are consecutive numbers after those of the shorter lengths. A set of lengths with more codes than there is room for is damage; one with fewer is taken, since RFC 1951 allows a single distance code of one bit, and none at all. A code of at most nine bits is found with one look in a table of 512 entries; a longer one bit by bit. So building a code costs at most a few hundred steps, however small the block: a file of many tiny blocks, each with codes of its own, does not pay for a large table each time. How long a whole file takes was not measured.
- **The fixed codes** (3.2.6): literals 0 to 143 in 8 bits, 144 to 255 in 9, 256 to 279 in 7, 280 to 287 in 8; distances in 5.
- **A block's own codes** (3.2.7): 257 to 286 literal and length codes (more is damage), 1 to 32 distance codes, their lengths given with a code of their own, in the order 16, 17, 18, 0, 8, 7, 9, 6, 10, 5, 11, 4, 12, 3, 13, 2, 14, 1, 15; 16 repeats the length before 3 to 6 times (there must be one before), 17 and 18 give 3 to 10 and 11 to 138 lengths of 0; a repeat may run on from the literal lengths into the distance lengths, but not past the last.
- **Copies** (3.2.5): lengths 3 to 258 and distances 1 to 32,768, from the tables of RFC 1951, written out in `unpack.rs`. Codes 286 and 287, and distance codes 30 and 31, have a place in the codes but never stand for anything: damage if they come. A copy from before the first byte is damage. The last 32 KB unpacked are always kept for copies to reach into; the bytes before them are handed on in pieces of 64 KB.

## JPEG

What it follows: ITU-T T.81 (the JPEG standard, also ISO/IEC 10918-1) and JFIF 1.02. The code: `src/jpeg.rs` (the segments, the frame, the scans and the dots), `src/entropy.rs` (the packed data: bits, Huffman codes, and the coefficients of each kind of scan) and `src/transform.rs` (the inverse transforms and the colours). The rules below were written down before the code, and the crate and the tests' second reader (`test-tools/jpeg_reference.py`) were both written from them.

- **What it reads:** baseline frames (SOF0); extended sequential (SOF1) and progressive (SOF2) frames with 8-bit samples, the progressive ones with spectral selection and successive approximation; all with Huffman codes; tables in any of their four places, and quantization tables of 8 or 16 bits, in any of these frames; a sequential photo in one scan or in several; restart markers; one component (grey) or three (colour); sampling factors from 1 to 4 whose ratios to the largest are whole (Hmax / Hi and Vmax / Vi whole numbers for every component): 4:4:4, 4:2:2, 4:2:0, 4:4:0, 4:1:1, and odder ones, with the first component subsampled too. In a frame of one component the sampling factors do not count (A.2.2).
- **What it turns down as a kind it does not read** (`it is a kind of JPEG I do not read: ...`): arithmetic coding; 12-bit samples; lossless and hierarchical frames; other than one or three components (CMYK and YCCK have four); sampling ratios that are not whole, such as 3 against 2; a height given only after the first scan (DNL, or a height of 0); and the markers T.81 keeps for later (JPG, JPGn and RES).
- **What it turns down as damaged** (`it is damaged: ...`), so that nothing is concealed and a photo is read whole or not at all: a segment whose length does not fit what it holds; bytes between segments that are not a marker; a second start marker, a second frame, a scan before the frame; a restart marker outside a scan's data; in the frame, a width of 0, no components, sampling factors outside 1 to 4, a table's place above 3, two components of one name; in the tables, a precision other than 8 or 16 bits, a 0 in a quantization table, a table of codes with more codes than can be; a scan that names no components or more than 4, one its frame does not have, its components out of the frame's order, units of more than 10 blocks, or a table not given yet; a component that comes in two sequential scans, or never by the end marker; in a progressive frame, a band that is not as T.81 allows, or scans that do not build its coefficients up in order; and in the data, a code its table does not have, a code that means nothing, a run of zeros past the end of its band, a run of empty blocks past its restart interval, a coefficient larger than 16 bits hold, fill bytes that no marker follows, data that ends before its last unit or goes on where a marker must come, and a restart marker missing or out of turn. A file cut anywhere before its end marker: `the file ends before the picture does`.
- **What it passes over:** APPn and COM segments, by their lengths, reading only JFIF's APP0 and Adobe's APP14 (see "Colours" below) and an Exif note's turn (APP1, see "The turn" below); DAC segments, which only arithmetic coding uses; the TEM marker; fill bytes (FF) before any marker (B.1.1.2); anything after the end marker. In a sequential frame a scan's band and point transforms (Ss, Se, Ah and Al) are not read: T.81 sets them to 0, 63, 0 and 0, and nothing depends on them.
- **The order:** the frame once, before the first scan; tables anywhere before the scan that uses them, and given again between scans if so made; a component's quantization table taken as it stands when the component's first scan begins, as the common readers take it.
- **Huffman codes** (B.2.4.2, Annex C) are built from a table's 16 counts and its values: the codes of each length are consecutive numbers, after those of the shorter lengths. The code of all 1-bits of any length is kept as the beginning of longer codes, so a table whose codes reach it, or that has more than 256 values, has more codes than can be. A code of at most 9 bits is found with one look in a table of 512 entries, a longer one length by length (F.2.2.3).
- **The packed data** (F.2.2.5, B.1.1.5) is read highest bit first. FF 00 stands for FF, and fill bytes may come only before a marker. At the end of a restart interval or of the scan, the bits left of the last byte are padding, whose value is not checked; no whole byte may be left before the marker, and a bit asked for past the data is damage.
- **Restart markers** (B.2.1, B.2.4.4): after every Ri units (the restart interval) comes the next of RST0 to RST7, in turn, RST0 first in each scan, and none after the last unit; then the DC predictions start afresh, and a run of empty blocks may not go on past the interval (G.1.2.2).
- **Coefficients** are decoded as F.2.2 (sequential) and G.1.2 (progressive) describe them, read backwards as G.2 says, and held in 16 bits each: a DC coefficient, once its difference is added and it is shifted by Al, must lie from -32768 to 32767, and an AC coefficient of a band's first scan, shifted by Al, from -32767 to 32767; the later scans cannot then carry either further, since each adds one bit below the last. A DC size above 15 means nothing, and so do, in a sequential scan, a size of 0 with a run other than 0 (the end of the block) and 15 (16 zeros), and, in a later scan, a size other than 0 and 1. 16 zeros that end exactly at the block's last coefficient are taken. The coefficients are multiplied by their quantization table's numbers only when they are turned into dots.
- **The progression** (G.1.1.1): a DC band holds the DC coefficient only, and only DC scans may hold several components; an AC band runs from Ss to Se, 1 to 63 at most, Ss no more than Se; Ah and Al are at most 13; a component's first DC scan comes before its AC scans; a band's first scan has Ah 0 and sends coefficients not sent before, and each later scan has Ah the last scan's Al and Al one less. A band never sent stays 0: a progressive photo whose last scans never came shows less detail, as other readers show it.
- **The size read at:** of n = 1, 2, 4 and 8 dots a block side (an eighth, a quarter, a half, the whole size), the smallest whose picture, ceil(X x n / 8) by ceil(Y x n / 8) dots, is still at least as large as the picture as shown (`fitted_size`); but a picture shown at its whole size is read whole, since for a photo of one dot an eighth would be as large, and its dot would be the block's average. The picture read at that size is then shrunk the rest of the way, as a PNG is ("Shrinking"). The last dots of a row or column at a reduced size may take in some of the samples the encoder added to fill its last blocks (A.2.4).
- **The inverse transforms,** from the dequantized coefficients F(v, u) of a block (row v, column u of the 8 by 8 block in its natural order, not the zig-zag order):
  - n = 1: the block's one dot is ((F(0, 0) + 4) >> 3) + 128, held to 0 to 255: the block's average, since T.81's first coefficient is eight times it. Here and below, >> shifts right rounding down, towards minus infinity.
  - n = 2, 4 and 8: s(x, y) = 1/4 x the sum, over u and v from 0 to n - 1, of C(u) C(v) F(v, u) cos((2x + 1) u pi / 2n) cos((2y + 1) v pi / 2n), with C(0) = 1 / sqrt(2) and C(u) = 1 otherwise, for x and y from 0 to n - 1. For n = 8 this is T.81's own inverse transform (A.3.3). For n = 4 and 2 it uses only the first 4 by 4 or 2 by 2 coefficients, and gives the same cosines at the centre of each 2 by 2 or 4 by 4 group of dots: the picture without its finest detail. T.81 does not say this; it is worked out from the transform.
  - In whole numbers: one table of 64 constants, K(u, x) = round(8192 x 1/2 x C(u) x cos((2x + 1) u pi / 16)) for u and x from 0 to 7: 2896 for u = 0, and for example 4017, 3406, 2276 and 799 for u = 1. At n dots a side the constants are K_n(u, x) = K(u x 8 / n, x), the same cosines. Rows first: t(v, x) = (the sum over u of K_n(u, x) x F(v, u), + 1024) >> 11, which keeps 2 bits more than the samples; then columns: s(x, y) = (the sum over v of K_n(v, y) x t(v, x), + 16384) >> 15; then + 128, held to 0 to 255. Every sum is made in 64 bits.
  - The tests check these against the exact transform, worked out in floating point, on 3,000 random blocks at each n: every dot is within one level, and was at most 0.71 off.
- **Colours:** grey: red, green and blue all Y. Three components are taken as the common readers take them, from the notes that come before the first scan: YCbCr when JFIF's APP0 segment has come (it begins `JFIF` and a zero byte); otherwise as Adobe's APP14 segment says, red, green and blue as they are for transform 0 and YCbCr for any other; otherwise red, green and blue when the components are named R, G and B (82, 71 and 66), and YCbCr when not. A photo whose notes disagree is shown as other programs show it. YCbCr is turned into red, green and blue by JFIF's formulas, R = Y + 1.402 (Cr - 128), G = Y - 0.34414 (Cb - 128) - 0.71414 (Cr - 128), B = Y + 1.772 (Cb - 128), in whole numbers: R = Y + ((91881 x (Cr - 128) + 32768) >> 16), G = Y + ((-22554 x (Cb - 128) - 46802 x (Cr - 128) + 32768) >> 16), B = Y + ((116130 x (Cb - 128) + 32768) >> 16), each held to 0 to 255 (the constants are JFIF's numbers times 65536, rounded). Colour profiles and Exif's notes about colour are not applied. Every dot is wholly there (alpha 255).
- **Subsampled colour:** the dot (x, y) of the picture read at n dots a block side takes component i's sample at (x x Hi / Hmax, y x Vi / Vmax), rounded down, so that each sample covers Hmax / Hi by Vmax / Vi dots. Other readers smooth colour that was subsampled, so a single dot at a colour edge can differ by many levels, though the averages over a few dots agree (see "How it is tested").
- **One scan, or several:** a picture whose first scan holds all its components (every baseline picture made in one pass, grey or colour) is read a row of units at a time and shrunk as it comes, as a PNG is. Any other, progressive or sequential in several scans, keeps, for every block of every component, its first n by n coefficients and one bit for each of its 64 saying whether it is non-zero yet (a later scan of successive approximation sends a correction bit for every coefficient that is, G.1.2.3); it is turned into dots, a row of units at a time, after its end marker. Those bytes, 8 + 2 x n x n a block, are counted against the caller's limit before anything is set aside.
- **The turn** (CIPA DC-008, Exif 3.1, read in the standard itself): a camera keeps a photo as its sensor saw it and notes how it was held, in an Exif note. That is an APP1 segment that begins with `Exif` and two zero bytes (4.7.2), then a TIFF head: its byte order (`II`, lowest byte first, or `MM`, highest first), 42, and where its 0th IFD begins, counted from the head's first byte. The IFD is a count of entries and then 12 bytes each: a tag, a type, a count and a value (4.6.2). The entry of tag 274, Orientation, of type SHORT and count 1 holds the turn, 1 to 8, in the first two bytes of its value (4.6.5.1.6). Only the first Exif note before the frame that gives a turn counts; a note that is not whole, or a turn outside 1 to 8, gives none, and the photo is shown as it is stored.
  - The dot (x, y) as seen comes from the stored picture of W by H dots at (Figure 13): 2: (W - 1 - x, y), mirrored left to right; 3: (W - 1 - x, H - 1 - y), a half turn; 4: (x, H - 1 - y), mirrored top to bottom; 5: (y, x); 6: (y, H - 1 - x), a quarter turn clockwise, as a phone held upright keeps its photos; 7: (W - 1 - y, H - 1 - x); 8: (W - 1 - y, x), a quarter turn the other way. 5 and 7 are 6 and 8 mirrored.
  - For 5 to 8 the sides swap: the room's sides are swapped before the size to read at is chosen, the photo is read and shrunk as it is stored, and the shrunk picture is turned last, so it fills the room as it is seen. The turn costs a second copy of the shown picture, for a moment.

## Shrinking

- **The size shown** (`fitted_size`): a picture that fits the room is shown as it is, and nothing is made larger. Otherwise it is made as large as fits, keeping its shape: if it is wider than the room's shape it fills the room's width, and its height is its height times the room's width divided by its width, rounded to the nearest dot; if it is taller, the other way round; a side is at least one dot. A photo of 1600 by 1200 dots in a page of the frame, 554 by 426 dots, is shown at 554 by 416; with a caption under it, in 554 by 406, at 541 by 406.
- **A picture that fits** passes through unchanged, dot for dot.
- **A shrunk picture:** dot x of a row falls on shown column x times the shown width divided by the width, rounded down, and row y on shown row y times the shown height divided by the height, rounded down. Each shown dot is the average of the dots that fall on it: its alpha the average of their alphas; its colour the average of their colours, each counted as much as its alpha, so that see-through dots do not darken their neighbours. Both are rounded to the nearest; a shown dot that only see-through dots fell on is black and see-through. Rows are summed as they come, one shown row at a time.

## Memory

Everything set aside for a picture is bounded before it is set aside: the picture's size, from its head, against the caller's limits, before anything is unpacked.

- the shown picture: its dots, four bytes each, never more than the picture's own;
- the sums of the shown row being summed: 40 bytes for each shown column;
- two rows of the file's bytes, each at most the picture's width times 8 bytes, and one row of dots, four bytes each;
- an interlaced picture whole, four bytes a dot, bounded by `interlaced_dots_most`;
- for unpacking: 96 KB of unpacked bytes and three small code tables;
- for a JPEG photo read as it comes: each component's samples for one row of units (its blocks across times n, by its v times n), one row of dots, one block's n by n coefficients, and its tables, at most eight Huffman tables of about 1.3 KB each;
- for one that keeps its blocks (a progressive photo, or a sequential one in several scans): 8 + 2 x n x n bytes a block, bounded by `held_most`;
- for a photo whose Exif note turns it: a second copy of the shown picture, while it is turned;
- the file itself, which is the caller's.

**Measured** (`tests/memory.rs`, on the computer the project is built on, with a counting allocator: the most held at once beyond what was held before):

| Picture | Shown at, in a room of 554 by 400 | Held at most | Of which the shown picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A head that says 30,000 by 30,000 | turned down for its size | 0 bytes | |
| 3000 by 2000 dots | 554 by 369 | 973,421 bytes | 817,704 |
| 554 by 400 dots | as it is | 995,497 bytes | 886,400 |
| 16,000 by 100 dots | 554 by 3 | 292,365 bytes | 6,648 |
| A baseline JPEG of 4000 by 3000 dots, 4:2:0 | 533 by 400 | 887,234 bytes | 852,800 |
| A baseline JPEG of 554 by 400 dots | as it is | 905,290 bytes | 886,400 |
| A baseline JPEG of 16,000 by 100 dots | 554 by 3 | 45,917 bytes | 6,648 |
| A progressive JPEG of 4000 by 3000 dots, 4:2:0 | 533 by 400, read at a quarter | 5,399,234 bytes | 852,800 |

So a PNG picture that is not interlaced costs, whatever its size, about the shown picture and a few hundred KB more, and a JPEG photo read as it comes the shown picture and some tens of KB. A JPEG photo that keeps its blocks costs, besides, 8 + 2 x n x n bytes for each: the 282,000 blocks of 4000 by 3000 dots in 4:2:0 take 4.5 MB at a quarter, 16 bytes each (measured above), 2.8 MB at an eighth and 38 MB at its whole size. An interlaced picture is held whole first, four bytes a dot, up to `interlaced_dots_most`: one of 1000 by 1000 dots holds 4 MB more (worked out; no interlaced picture was measured). The kernel's heap gives each piece a power of two in size and never gives frames back (see [memory.md](memory.md)): the shown picture of the usual frame takes a piece of 1 MB.

How long a picture takes to read where the kernel runs: see "In the kernel" below.

## In the kernel

Code: `file_picture` in `src/files.rs`, which reads a kept PNG picture or JPEG photo whole from the room for files and calls `png_picture` or `jpeg_picture` with the room a page of the frame has; the layout and painting in `src/drawing.rs` (see "Pictures" in [screen.md](screen.md)).

- **The limits** (`PICTURE_LIMITS` in `files.rs`): at most 9,000,000 dots in all, enough for a screenshot of 3840 by 2160 (8,294,400); at most 16,384 on either side; and at most 4,000,000 in an interlaced picture, enough for 2560 by 1440, which is held whole first, four bytes a dot, so 16 MB. The file itself is at most 4 MB, as every kept file. A picture over them is turned down at its head, at once: `it is 3000 by 3001 dots, more than I show`.
- **Time, measured in QEMU** on the computer the project is built on (2026-11-24, with the timer's ticks of 10 ms, in a build with lines of its own that said them, since taken out): reading the file from the disk, about 0.26 s a MB (1.8 MB in 0.49 s, 2.9 MB in 0.77 s). Reading the picture: 1600 by 1200 dots of 8-bit colour, packed into 1.8 MB, 0.73 s; 1100 by 900 dots of noise, 2.9 MB, 0.31 s; 4000 by 4000 dots of one 8-bit colour, 4.5 s; the same in 16-bit colour and alpha, 8.7 s; 1414 by 1414 interlaced, 16-bit colour and alpha, 1.2 s; 2000 by 2000 interlaced, at the limit of 4,000,000 dots and held whole, 16 MB, 2.2 s in 16-bit colour and alpha and 1.1 s in 8-bit colour. So at the limit of 9,000,000 dots the slowest kind takes about 5 s and an 8-bit one about 2.5 s; the usual pictures of the web take well under 2 s, the file's reading included. Painting a page with a picture of 554 by 416 dots took about 0.2 s, the frame alone 0.1 s. The main loop waits meanwhile: keys wait in the keyboard's line, and the network's task goes on. A document reads at most 4 pictures, so it waits some 20 s at the very worst.
- **Memory in the kernel:** the file read whole, a piece of the heap of up to 4 MB, given back once the picture is read; what the crate holds while it reads (above; an interlaced picture up to 16 MB, a progressive photo's blocks up to 16 MB); and each shown picture, kept while its document is shown, under 1 MB, a piece of 1 MB. The heap hands a piece that was given back out again for the next request of its size (see [memory.md](memory.md)), so opening pictures again and again takes no more than the largest at once.
- **The limits of a JPEG photo** (`JPEG_LIMITS` in `files.rs`): at most 100,000,000 dots in all, at most 65,535 on either side, as many as the format can say, and at most 16 MB for the blocks of a photo that keeps them until its last scan (a progressive one). The time a photo takes follows its data, which the 4 MB a kept file may have bounds, more than its dots, so the limit on dots can be wide. A photo over them is turned down at its head: `it is 10000 by 10001 dots, more than I show`; a progressive one whose blocks would take more is turned down before they are set aside, in the same words.
- **Time of a JPEG photo, measured in QEMU** in the same way (2026-11-24), shown in a page of the frame: Windows' own wallpapers, a baseline 6400 by 4000 dots in 1.4 MB, 0.90 s, read at an eighth; a baseline 3840 by 2400 in 1.6 MB, 0.84 s, read at a quarter; progressive photos of 3840 by 2160 in 2.1 MB, 1.14 s, and 2736 by 1539 in 0.6 MB, 0.45 s. Made-up ones at the edges: 10000 by 10000 dots, every block flat, 1.29 s; 8000 by 6000, 0.64 s, and the same progressive, holding 11 MB of blocks, 0.42 s; 1600 by 1600 dots whose every coefficient is non-zero, 1.6 MB of data, 0.45 s. Reading the file from the disk took about 0.16 s a MB, and painting the page about 0.22 s. A progressive photo of 10000 by 10000 dots was turned down at once: its blocks would take 23 MB. So a photo within the limits takes at most about 3 to 4 s, the 4 MB file's reading from the disk included, and the usual photos of a phone about 1 to 2 s.
- **Nothing of a picture goes to the model,** nor is anything of it written to the disk but the file as it was fetched.

## How it is tested

The crate's tests run on the computer the project is built on, not in QEMU: it needs no machine. They are built and run as a program there, with the Visual Studio linker that cargo finds for `x86_64-pc-windows-msvc`; a computer whose settings refuse programs that were built a moment ago cannot run them. GitHub runs them in the build check (`build.yml`), which every pull request must pass, and the runner's table (`suites.py`) has two entries for the crate, "pictures" and "pictures breaks", which a change to the crate or its test data picks, and nothing that boots. Since the kernel builds the crate in, such a change also picks the entries that boot the kernel and show pictures, "picture documents" (see [testing.md](testing.md)).

- **`python test_pictures.py`** (in `test-tools/`): `cargo test` (the tests in `src/tests.rs` and `src/jpeg_tests.rs`, and the count of memory), `cargo clippy` with every warning an error (passed over, and said so, where clippy is not installed), and `cargo build` for the kernel's own target, which must give no warning. Cargo's output goes to the tests' work folder, never into the project, and a `Cargo.lock` that cargo writes beside the crate is removed again. Its last line is `FAILURES: none`, or the steps that failed.
- **`python test_pictures.py --cross-check`** also reads, with the crate and with a second reader written apart from it in Python (`test-tools/png_reference.py`, which unpacks with Python's own zlib), the PNG pictures that come with Python, with Git and with QEMU on that computer, QEMU's many icons sampled, and compares their dots one by one. Read only; nothing of them is kept. These pictures were made by other programs, so they are the check that does not rest on this project's own reading of the rules. On the computer it was run on, 106 were compared, every dot the same: 74 red-green-blue with alpha, 13 red-green-blue, 12 palettes of 8 bits and 3 of 4 bits (14 of them with see-through colours), 2 greys of 8 bits, 1 of 1 bit, and 1 grey with alpha, all of 8 bits but those. None was interlaced, of 16 bits, a grey of 2 or 4 bits, a palette of 1 or 2 bits, or had a see-through grey or colour: those are checked against the project's own second reader only.
- **The JPEG cross-check,** in the same run: the JPEG files of Windows' own folders of wallpapers and of apps (`Web`, `SystemApps`) and of the programs installed (`Program Files`), and the sample photos fetched once into the tests' work folder, if they are there (Go's test photos and a set of photos turned each of Exif's eight ways), each read once however often it is found and at most 16 MB, are read by the crate and by Windows' own reader (WIC, through PowerShell, with colour profiles left out). WIC writes each photo's dots, a batch of at most 100 million dots at a time, which is removed once the crate's test (`jpeg_cross_check`) has compared it. Two sound readers differ a little (T.81 allows it, A.3.3), and more where colour was subsampled, since WIC smooths it; so what is compared is what smoothing does not move: at the whole size, the averages of squares of 16 by 16 dots, and the brightness of every dot that neither reader clipped to 0 or 255, in a grey photo or a YCbCr one whose Y is not subsampled ((299 red + 587 green + 114 blue) / 1000 gives Y back whatever the colour); and at a frame's size (a room of 554 by 400), the averages of squares, WIC's dots brought to the same grid by averaging and shrunk the same way. A photo WIC reads may be turned down only as a kind the crate does not read, or as no JPEG at all. WIC gives a photo's dots as they are stored, so one whose Exif note turns it is compared with WIC's dots turned the same way: this checks the reading, and the samples below the turn. On the computer it was run on, 137 photos were found, of every kind found there: baseline and progressive, grey and colour, 4:4:4, 4:2:2, 4:2:0, 4:4:0, 4:1:1, 4:1:0 and odder layouts, red-green-blue, with restart markers and without, up to 6400 by 4000 dots. 136 were compared and one, CMYK, turned down as a kind not read. At the whole size the squares were at most 3.38 apart and the brightness of a dot at most 2; at a frame's size the squares were at most 7.62 apart, in a strongly coloured part of a photo read at a quarter, where WIC clips more dots to 0 than the crate does, which raises its averages. The test allows 4, 3 and 10. A brightness check on all of a photo's dots, clipped ones too, differs by up to 55 levels in Go's odd layouts, where Y itself is subsampled and WIC smooths it. Run again with the turn (2026-11-25): the same 137 photos were found and 136 compared, the eight turned samples among them, compared turned, with the same worst figures, and the same CMYK sample turned down.
- **The turn, against photos made by others** (`jpeg_turn_samples`, in the same run, if the samples were fetched): a public set of eight photos made to show Exif's turns, one landscape stored in each of the eight ways with its turn in its Exif note. Each is read in a room of 300 by 300 dots and turned as its note says, and must look like the first, which is stored as it is seen: the averages of their squares of 16 by 16 dots at most 10 apart, the middle left out, where each has its own number painted large. They were at most 7.31 apart (the eight were packed apart, so their dots differ a little). This is the check of the turn that does not rest on this project's own reading of Exif.
- **The test photos** were made by `test-tools/make_jpeg_cases.py` with a JPEG writer of the project's own (`test-tools/jpeg_reference.py`, which codes the scans as T.81's Annexes F and G say and makes its Huffman tables as Annex K does, or on purpose with codes of up to 16 bits). Their blocks come from samples of slopes, discs and noise put through the forward transform and quantized with T.81's example tables; their dots were worked out from the blocks by the rules above, and each photo was read back by the second reader, which had to find the same blocks and give the same dots. Windows' reader read all of them but two, and the averages of their squares of 16 by 16 dots agreed within 8.42, the most in the two photos whose coefficients are as large as 16 bits hold, a turned photo compared with WIC's dots turned the same way; that is how a photo of one dot, read at an eighth, was found to show its block's average, and a test photo's Exif note to lack the end of its directory. The two it turns down are damaged on purpose: an Exif note cut short, and a made-up XMP packet that is not whole, both of which it takes as a damaged photo, where the crate passes over a note it cannot use (see "The turn"). They lie in `test-data/jpeg-cases.bin`, 186 files in 511,659 bytes, listed in `test-data/jpeg-cases.txt`: 47 photos read whole, 46 shown in smaller rooms, and 46 that must be turned down, each with the words it must be turned down with. They cover baseline, extended and progressive frames; grey (with sampling factors that do not count), 4:4:4, 4:2:2, 4:4:0, 4:1:1, 3:1:1, 4:2:0, a mix of 2 by 2, 2 by 1 and 1 by 2, and one whose Y is subsampled; a sequential photo in two and in three scans, in the frame's order and not; restart intervals of 1 to 9 units, in sequential and progressive scans; a progression as common encoders send it, and one whose last scans never come; tables in all four places, 16-bit quantization tables, codes of up to 16 bits; red-green-blue by Adobe's note and by the components' names, and the notes disagreeing; other segments, fill bytes and bytes after the end; coefficients as large as 16 bits hold, with tables of 65535; photos of 1 by 1, 8 by 8 and 9 by 9 dots; and a photo of 37 by 21 dots stored in each of Exif's eight turns, read whole and in two rooms that make it shrink, its note highest byte first too, a progressive one turned, and notes that give no turn: a turn of 9 and a note cut short; and an APP1 that is not Exif's before the one that is, which must not hide its turn.
- **The test pictures and streams** were made by `test-tools/make_picture_cases.py` with Python's zlib, and each picture was read back by the second reader before it was kept. They lie in `test-data/cases.bin`, one after another, 86 files in 540,110 bytes, and `test-data/cases.txt` lists the 56 cases and where each file lies: two files rather than a hundred, so that the runner's table can name them. They are kept as made, since another version of zlib may pack the same bytes differently, and git is told to leave them as they are (`test-data/.gitattributes`). They cover every kind of colour and depth, palettes with and without transparency, see-through greys and colours, Adam7 (with passes left out), every filter, stored, fixed, dynamic and Huffman-only blocks, runs, a 512-byte window, packed dots cut into parts of 0 to 7 bytes, parts that may be passed over, bytes after the end, and streams of up to 205 KB unpacked, with copies reaching back 32,000 bytes.
- **What the JPEG tests check:** every test photo read whole and in rooms smaller than it, dot for dot with the second reader, and its head, its turn and its size as seen; each refusal with its own words; codes of every length from 1 to 16, FF 00, fill bytes, and data that ends at a marker and at the end of the file, sequential blocks, a first progressive scan's runs of empty blocks and a refinement's correction bits, worked out by hand bit by bit; the transforms within one level of the exact on 3,000 random blocks at each size; an eighth's rounding and the colours by hand; the limits, `held_most` to the byte; and damage that must never stop it: files cut at every byte, which must say `the file ends before the picture does`, and any byte of seven photos changed.
- **What the PNG tests check:** every test picture read whole, dot for dot, and shown in rooms smaller than it, dot for dot with the second reader's shrinking; sizes and averages worked out by hand; every length from 3 to 258 and the edges of every distance code, 32,768 among them, in a stream made by the tests themselves, unpacking past the place where bytes are handed on; each rule above turned down with its own words; and damage at three depths, which must never stop it: any byte of a file changed (turned down by a check value), any byte of a part's data changed with its check value made right again, and the unpacked rows themselves changed and packed again; files cut at every byte; streams cut and changed at hundreds of places.
- **Breaks on purpose:** `python run_picture_breaks.py` puts each of 37 mistakes into a copy of the crate and runs the one test that must catch it, 20 of them in the JPEG reader, three of those in the turn (turn 6 taken as 8, the room's sides not swapped for a quarter turn, and an Exif note's byte order taken the wrong way); all were caught. Three of the PNG ones were missed at first and taught something: a change to Paeth's first tie changed nothing (a tie between the left byte and the one above comes only when the two are equal) and was replaced by its second tie; no copy reached back the full 32,768 bytes right where unpacked bytes are handed on, so the test now lands one exactly there; and the limit on literal codes said the same words as another check, so it got words of its own. One break reads codes longer than nine bits wrong, to show that the test streams have such codes.

**What was run** (on the computer the project is built on): 32 tests and the count of memory passed, clippy gave no warning, the build for the kernel's target gave no warning, the cross-checks compared 106 PNG pictures and 136 JPEG photos, the eight photos of Exif's turns, each turned as its note says, were at most 7.31 off the first, and 37 of 37 breaks were caught (2026-11-25, with the turn).
